# 18. Tried and dropped

**When** marks the week of our six-week effort in which we tested or decided an idea. **Outcome** records what ruled it out: *measured*, *modelled*, or *replayed* and rejected; *shipped then replaced* or *removed*; *folded into another change*; or *rejected on principle*. The final column notes what replaced it or what must change before retrying. In this public copy, our internal PR and run links are omitted; your own register should include an `Evidence` column matching the template in [`ci-read-what-was-tried`](../../skills/ci-doctor/references/checks/ci-read-what-was-tried.md).

| When | Idea | Outcome | What we saw | Lesson | Replaced by, or retry when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Local-only pre-merge checks, no CI | Shipped then replaced | Runner spend dropped to zero during the interval, with no controlled total-cost or reliability comparison recorded | Zero CI bill isn't a measured saving | Dedicated-runner CI |
| Weeks 1–2 | Many tiny parallel jobs (39) | Measured and rejected | Faster completion, but substantially more rounded runner minutes than packed jobs | Include setup and billing rounding in the model | Fewer, fuller jobs; retry only if setup and rounding stop dominating |
| Week 2 | Arm runners for JS tests | Measured and rejected | Tests ran ~30% slower and three integration files failed, despite ~37% cheaper per-minute pricing | Unit price isn't service price | Keep Arm for native builds; retry when the same tests match speed and pass |
| Week 2 | Specialized checkout cache (mirrored checkout) | Rejected | Standard checkout was already cheap, while the mirror billed persistent storage | Measure fixed work before adding paid storage | Repos with multi-GB checkouts |
| Week 2 | Docker layer caching for clean, verified builds | Rejected | Conflicted with our clean-build contract; verified images were already reused by content tag | Reuse only independently verified complete images | — |
| Weeks 2 and 4 | Dependency disk holding only `node_modules` | Measured and rejected | Installs and postinstalls still ran because their markers lived in the store outside the disk | Preserve and verify the complete install: toolchain, store markers, and workspace links | Complete-install disk ([section 6](06-reuse-work-by-its-real-inputs.md#setup-caches-on-blacksmith)) |
| Week 2 | Vitest isolation, pools, and module caches | Measured and rejected | Shards spent nearly all their time inside the tests themselves | Bottlenecks were single slow files and waits, not runner overhead | Retry when import or transform time is a real share of shard time |
| Week 2 | One job per test file (or per part of a file) | Measured and rejected | Each slice used roughly one core in four | Per-job setup and minute rounding dominate | Never, while setup is billed per job |
| Week 2 | Vitest `--changed` / import-graph selection over a whole-Worker harness | Replayed and rejected | Selected 494 of 1,035 files for one edit; median 19% fewer files but ~0 billed minutes saved; missed 13 disk readers; slow to compute | Static imports don't capture integration coverage | Near selection + sweeps + explicit coverage edges (section 4) |
| Week 2 | Automatic input tracking for every build | Measured and rejected (for builds) | Hit `spawn EBUSY` on our dev platform; subprocess reads raised correctness concerns too | Use explicit build inputs guarded by a completeness lint rule | Retry when tracking works on every dev platform; tests use it today (section 6) |
| Week 2 | Time-balanced shards before splitting giant files | Modelled and deferred | Can't balance shards while one file exceeds a shard's share | Split giant files first, then balance | Shipped after splitting long files (section 7) |
| Week 2 | 9 workers on 8 vCPU | Measured and rejected | Took 1.5–1.8× longer than 6 workers, with timing failures | Spare CPU isn't spare capacity | — |
| Week 2 | Ordinary tests after every merge | Folded into another change | Closed proposal, superseded before measurement | Batch broad checks; keep per-merge type checks | Sweeps (section 5) |
| Week 3 | Halving a timer-heavy job's runner | Measured and rejected | Each file ran 30–45% slower with saturated CPU for only a small saving | Keep burst headroom | — |
| Week 3 | Shared-machine Docker admission | Shipped then removed | Dedicated CI replaced the shared box; removing the forwarding socket hung most runs, so it stayed | A removal can depend on an undocumented dependency | — |
| Week 3 | Human-picked extra slow/sandbox coverage on PRs | Shipped then removed | Extra catches were all flakes; zero real failures across 81 slow jobs | Broad pre-merge coverage must prove observed value | Changed files on PRs, rest in sweeps |
| Week 4 | Rebuilding macOS release binaries less often | Modelled and rejected | Every macOS job was a real release; binaries embed their version and the service pairs only with its own release | Unchanged source doesn't make an old binary valid for a new release | Fewer releases, or an intentional version-contract redesign |
| Week 4 | 10× faster test clock to get a shard file under 90 s | Measured and rejected | One case lacked enough real time for its work and flaked in CI | Shorten a deadline only when remaining real time still covers work on a busy runner | Real-clock deadlines sized to actual work |
| Week 4 | Long-lived binaries and tarballs as artifacts | Shipped then replaced | Exhausted artifact storage and broke sweeps | Match retention to usefulness | Cache handoffs and short retention (a later outage exposed what still relied on artifacts, section 19) |
| Week 4 | Staging-connected recording/proof extension | Shipped then reverted | Landed, reverted, and remaining hooks removed | A closed or reverted experiment alone doesn't explain why it failed | Deterministic recording/replay stayed; revert reason wasn't recorded |
| Week 4 | Reusing imported state across server files | Measured and rejected | State leaks failed 8 tests across 6 files on a second run | A single green run doesn't prove isolation | — |
| Week 4 | Lower Go memory limits for the type checker | Measured and rejected | 50–80% slower to save <2 GB | Live heap sets the floor | Go backend only; since replaced by `tsrs` (section 9) |
| Week 4 | Trimming what TypeScript loads to speed up checking | Measured, kept, no speed-up | Fewer declarations loaded, but checking time didn't move | Scheduling, not loading, set wall time | Retry when the checker becomes load-bound |
| Week 4 | Splitting test files further to shorten shards | Rejected | Every extra file pays another harness start | Split only files past the per-file limit | — |
| Weeks 4–5 | Whole-tree hash test-pass cache | Shipped then replaced | Replayed 0 of 4,872 file runs across 7 sweeps | Key by what each task actually reads | Tracked-read tasks (section 6) |
| Weeks 4–6 | Rust in CI: `lld`/`mold`, 256 codegen units, opt-level tweaks, one combined test binary, `sccache`, shared compile with packaging | Measured and rejected | Negligible, slower, or lost fidelity; combined binary broke 3 tests | Profile before tuning the compiler | — |
| Undated | Shared Cargo `target/` dir across worktrees | Rejected | Returned another worktree's binary as "fresh" | mtime freshness isn't content identity | Per-worktree `target/` + `sccache` |
| Weeks 4–6 | Shared runtime / watch mode across test files | Measured and rejected | Saved <10% per file and broke isolation (module state, alarms, DB names) | Share compilation, not runtime state | — |
| Weeks 4–6 | Resident incremental bundler | Measured and rejected | Roughly halved bundling, but held 1.7 GB idle and got an agent OOM-killed | Memory is a real cost on a shared host | Bundle once per input state (section 8) |
| Week 4 | Deep property tests on every PR | Shipped then moved | Finished last while repeating fixed seeds PR runs already covered | Depth belongs in the sweep | Sweep runs with fresh seeds (section 5) |
| Week 4 | Caching only tiny packages on one shard | Measured and rejected | Five CI trials saved zero billed minutes because cached work was too small | Compare lookup/restore cost against removed work | Broader caching after independent lanes (section 6) |
| Week 4 | Cutting the rest of harness startup (no proxy, lazy wasm, code cache, shared/pooled runtime) | Measured and rejected | Remaining trimmable time while keeping per-file isolation was only a few percent of test time | Tests need the proxy, and a shared runtime breaks isolation | Making the server bundle load less at startup (a production change) |
| Week 4 | Cutting a shard's fixed costs (web build, test bundles, module cache) | Measured and rejected | Only seconds per shard; module cache keyed on lockfile and configs, so nearly every full run started cold | Check how often cache-key inputs actually change | A key that survives lockfile churn |
| Week 4 | Claim queue's fixed share, cross-run claims, time-only batches | Modelled and in CI; replaced | A slower runner outran the others' share; per-batch invocations repaid startup; time-only batches ignored per-file startup | Model both the slowest runner and per-file startup | Updated fixed share and per-file startup cost |
| Week 5 | Merging a declaration and cached-check spike wholesale | Folded into another change | Closed unmerged; project-level caching was built locally first | A closed spike isn't a rejected technique | — |
| Week 5 | Caching sub-second generation | Shipped then removed | Replay took 2–4× longer than regenerating | Cache overhead can exceed the work | Ordered direct generation writing only on change |
| Week 5 | npm tarball cache | Measured and rejected | Restoring 352 MB cost more than it saved | Always measure restore time | — |
| Week 5 | Duplicating remote cache in Actions cache | Shipped then removed | Hid whether remote reuse actually worked | Keep one cache layer so hits are attributable | Remote cache alone |
| Week 5 | Cached execution in rounds, two workers per group | Measured and replaced | Workers idled waiting for slow group members | Synchronized rounds idle workers | Independent lanes, one server file per task |
| Week 5 | One private cache path per lane | Measured and replaced | The path entered every key, preventing any cross-sweep replay | Anything in the environment can leak into a key | Shared storage with per-invocation status |
| Week 5 | Raising cache concurrency further | Rejected | Buffers exceeded the Worker's memory limit | Concurrency is memory-bound, not just CPU-bound | Shared byte budget instead of fixed buffers |
| Week 5 | Several broad local cached suites at once on a shared box | Measured and replaced | Warm single runs didn't hold up under concurrency: timeouts and resets | A shared-host limit, not an argument against broad checks | Fewer test slots and focused local selection |
| Week 5 | Four server files per cached task | Measured and rejected | Fewer launches, but longer shards and far more tail idle time | Coarse indivisible groups lose to fine-grained lanes | — |
| Week 5 | Fully async SQL test transport / deferred writes | Measured and rejected | Altered completion order and visibility, breaking clock-driven tests | Optimize transport while preserving caller semantics | Synchronous socket (section 10) |
| Undated | Cheap external sandboxes as runners | Measured and rejected | Roughly a third slower on average, and flaky | Don't trade every-PR wait time for pennies | — |
| By week 2 | Long-lived test boxes | Rejected | Idle time billed up to a 30-minute timeout | Lifecycle economics matter | — |
| — | A separate "fast" local command | Rejected on principle | — | Make the default command fast | — |
| — | A test mode in production code | Rejected on principle | — | Keep one code path; vary periods via settings | — |
| — | Making a fake slow to mimic a real rate limit | Rejected on principle | — | Prove behavior at the limit instead | — |
| — | Widening a production API to remove a test's last wait | Rejected on principle | — | The API is the product | — |

Rows folded into another change or closed before measurement are not failed optimizations—don't treat them as performance failures without evidence.
