# 18. Tried and dropped

**When** is the week of our six-week effort in which we tried or decided it. **Outcome** says what kind of evidence ruled it out: *measured*, *modelled* or *replayed* and rejected; *shipped then replaced* or *removed*; *folded into another change*; or *rejected on principle*. The last column says what replaced it, or what would have to change before trying again. Our rows' evidence was our own PRs and runs, which this public copy leaves out; your register keeps an Evidence column, as the [`ci-read-what-was-tried`](../../checks/ci-read-what-was-tried.md) check's template has.

| When | Idea | Outcome | What we saw | Lesson | Replaced by, or retry when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Local-only pre-merge checks, no CI | Shipped then replaced | Runner spend stopped for the interval; no controlled total-cost or reliability comparison was recorded | No CI bill isn't a measured saving | Dedicated-runner CI |
| Weeks 1–2 | Many tiny parallel jobs (39) | Measured and rejected | Faster completion, but substantially more rounded runner usage than packed jobs | Include setup and billing rounding in the model | Fewer, fuller jobs; retry only if setup and rounding stop dominating |
| Week 2 | Arm runners for JS tests | Measured and rejected | Tests ran ~30% slower and three integration files failed, despite a ~37% cheaper minute | Unit price isn't service price | Arm stays useful for native builds; retry when the same tests run as fast and pass |
| Week 2 | Specialized checkout cache (a mirrored checkout) | Rejected | The existing checkout was already cheap, and the mirror bills storage | Measure fixed work before adding persistent storage | Repos whose checkout is several GB |
| Week 2 | Docker layer caching for intentionally clean, verified builds | Rejected | Conflicted with the clean-build contract; verified images were already reused by content tag | Reuse only independently verified complete images | — |
| Weeks 2 and 4 | Dependency disk containing only `node_modules` | Measured and rejected | Installation and postinstall work remained, because their markers lived in the store outside the disk | Preserve and verify the complete installation, including toolchain, store markers and workspace links | The complete-install disk ([section 6](06-reuse-work-by-its-real-inputs.md#setup-caches-on-blacksmith)) |
| Week 2 | Vitest's isolation, pools and module caches | Measured and rejected | Shards spent almost all their time in the tests themselves | Time was in single slow files and tests that wait, not in the runner | Retry when import or transform time is a real share of a shard |
| Week 2 | One job per test file, or per part of one | Measured and rejected | Each part used about one core in four | Per-job setup and minute rounding dominate | Never, while setup is billed per job |
| Week 2 | Vitest `--changed` / import-graph selection over a whole-Worker harness | Replayed and rejected | 494 of 1,035 files for one edit; median 19% fewer files but ~0 billed minutes saved; 13 missed disk readers; slow to select | Imports don't describe integration coverage | Near selection plus sweeps, then explicit coverage edges (section 4) |
| Week 2 | Automatic input tracking for every build | Measured and rejected, for builds | `spawn EBUSY` on our dev platform; subprocess reads were a correctness concern too | Explicit inputs, with a lint rule for completeness | Retry when tracking works on every dev platform; tests use it today (section 6) |
| Week 2 | Time-balanced shards before splitting giant files | Modelled and deferred | Can't help while one file is longer than a shard's share | Split first, then balance | Shipped once the long files were split (section 7) |
| Week 2 | 9 workers on 8 vCPU | Measured and rejected | About 1.5–1.8× as long as 6 workers, with timing failures | Spare CPU isn't spare capacity | — |
| Week 2 | Ordinary tests after every merge | Folded into another change | A closed proposal, superseded before it was measured | Batch the broad check, keep merge types | Sweeps (section 5) |
| Week 3 | Halving a timer-heavy job's runner | Measured and rejected | Each file 30–45% slower, and CPU saturated, for a small saving | Keep burst headroom | — |
| Week 3 | Shared-machine Docker admission | Shipped then removed | Dedicated CI replaced the shared machine; removing the forwarding socket hung most runs, so it stayed | A removal can depend on something no one recorded | — |
| Week 3 | Human-picked extra slow/sandbox coverage on PRs | Shipped then removed | Extra catches were all flakes; zero failures in 81 slow jobs | Broad pre-merge coverage needs observed value | Changed files on PRs, the rest in sweeps |
| Week 4 | Rebuilding macOS release binaries less often | Modelled and rejected | Every macOS job was a real release; each binary embeds its version, and the service pairs only with its own release | Unchanged source doesn't make an old binary valid for a new release | Fewer releases, or a deliberately redesigned version contract |
| Week 4 | A test clock ten times faster to bring a shard file under 90 s | Measured and rejected | One case had too little real time for its work and flaked in CI | Shorten a deadline only where the real time left covers the work on a busy runner | Real-clock deadlines sized to the work |
| Week 4 | Long-lived binaries and tarballs as artifacts | Shipped then replaced | Exhausted artifact storage and broke sweeps | Retention follows usefulness | Cache handoffs and short retention; a later outage showed what still depended on artifacts (section 19) |
| Week 4 | Staging-connected recording/proof extension | Shipped then reverted | Landed, then reverted, and its remaining connections removed | A closed or reverted experiment doesn't establish why it was rejected | Deterministic recording and replay remained; the reason for the revert isn't recorded |
| Week 4 | Reusing imported state across server files | Measured and rejected | Leaks failed 8 tests in 6 files on a second run | A green run can't prove isolation | — |
| Week 4 | Lower Go memory limits for the type checker | Measured and rejected | 50–80% slower, <2 GB saved | The live heap is the floor | Go backend only; since replaced by tsrs (section 9) |
| Week 4 | Trimming what TypeScript loads, to speed up checking | Measured, kept, no speed-up | Declarations loaded fell; checking time didn't move | Scheduling set the time, not load | Retry when the checker is load-bound |
| Week 4 | Splitting test files further to shorten shards | Rejected | Each file adds a harness start | Split only what's past the per-file limit | — |
| Weeks 4–5 | Whole-tree hash test-pass cache | Shipped then replaced | Replayed 0 of 4,872 file runs over 7 sweeps | Key by what each task reads | Tracked-read tasks (section 6) |
| Weeks 4–6 | Rust in CI: lld/mold, 256 codegen units, opt-level tweaks, one combined test binary, sccache, shared compile with packaging | Measured and rejected | Negligible, slower, or lost fidelity; the combined binary broke 3 tests | Profile before tuning the compiler | — |
| Undated | Shared Cargo target dir across worktrees | Rejected | Returned the wrong worktree's binary as fresh | mtime freshness isn't content | One `target/` per worktree, plus sccache |
| Weeks 4–6 | Shared runtime / watch mode across test files | Measured and rejected | <10% a file; broke isolation (module state, alarms, DB names) | Share compilation, not state | — |
| Weeks 4–6 | A resident incremental bundler | Measured and rejected | About halved bundling, but held 1.7 GB idle and got an agent OOM-killed | Memory is a cost on a shared box | Bundle once per input state (section 8) |
| Week 4 | Deep property tests on every PR | Shipped then moved | Ended last, repeating fixed seeds the PR run covered | Depth belongs in the sweep | Sweep runs with fresh seeds (section 5) |
| Week 4 | Cache only tiny packages on one shard | Measured and rejected | Five CI trials saved no billed minutes, because the cached work was too small | Compare lookup and restore cost with the work removed | Broader caching after independent lanes (section 6) |
| Week 4 | Cutting the rest of a harness's start: no proxy, lazy wasm, a code cache, a shared or pooled runtime | Measured and rejected | What could go while each file keeps its own runtime was a few percent of test time | Tests need the proxy, and a shared runtime breaks per-file state | The server bundle loading less at start, a production change |
| Week 4 | Cutting a shard's fixed costs: the web build, test bundles, the module cache | Measured and rejected | Each was seconds a shard; the module cache's key took the lockfile and configs, so almost every full run started cold | Check a cache's key against how often its inputs change | A key that survives lockfile churn |
| Week 4 | The claim queue's fixed share, claims between runs, batches cut by time alone | Modelled and in CI; replaced | A slower machine ran past the others' share; per-batch runs paid start-up again; time-only batches ignored each file's start | Model the slowest machine and per-file start | Share and file-start cost went in |
| Week 5 | Merging a declaration and cached-check spike wholesale | Folded into another change | Closed unmerged; project-level caching was built locally first | A closed spike isn't a rejected technique | — |
| Week 5 | Caching sub-second generation | Shipped then removed | Replay took two to four times as long as the work | Cache overhead can beat the work | Ordered direct generation that writes only changes |
| Week 5 | npm tarball cache | Measured and rejected | The restore cost more than it saved | Measure the restore | — |
| Week 5 | The remote cache duplicated in the Actions cache | Shipped then removed | Hid whether remote reuse worked at all | Keep one copy, so hits are attributable | The remote cache alone |
| Week 5 | Cached execution in rounds, two workers per group | Measured and replaced | Workers waited for slow group members | Rounds idle workers | Independent lanes, one server file per task |
| Week 5 | One private cache path per lane | Measured and replaced | The path itself entered every key, so nothing replayed across sweeps | Anything in the environment can enter a key | Shared storage with per-invocation status |
| Week 5 | Raising cache concurrency further | Rejected | The buffers exceeded the Worker's memory allowance | Concurrency is bounded by memory, not only by the CPU | A shared byte budget instead of fixed buffers |
| Week 5 | Several broad local cached suites at once on a shared machine | Measured and replaced | Warm single runs didn't survive several together: timeouts and resets | A shared-host result, not a rule against broad checks | Fewer slots and focused local selection |
| Week 5 | Four server files per cached task | Measured and rejected | Fewer launches, but longer shards and much more idle time at the end | Indivisible groups lose to lanes | — |
| Week 5 | Fully async SQL test transport, or deferred writes | Measured and rejected | Changed completion order and visibility, breaking clock-driven cases | Optimize transport while preserving the caller's contract | The synchronous socket (section 10) |
| Undated | Cheap external sandboxes as runners | Measured and rejected | About a third slower on average, and flaky | Don't trade every-PR wait for cents | — |
| By week 2 | Long-lived test boxes | Rejected | Idle time billed up to a 30-minute timeout | Lifecycle economics matter | — |
| — | A separate "fast" local command | Rejected on principle | — | Make the default command fast | — |
| — | A test mode in production code | Rejected on principle | — | One code path; a period differs by setting | — |
| — | Making a fake slow to mimic a real rate limit | Rejected on principle | — | Prove behaviour at the limit instead | — |
| — | Widening a production API to remove a test's last wait | Rejected on principle | — | The API is the product | — |

Rows that were folded into another change, or closed before anyone measured them, aren't failed optimizations. Don't count them as performance failures unless evidence shows one.
